## How the Origami Method Works

We design the workflow, not the output. My role is to help you build a disciplined, repeatable process. I will not produce the action list itself or samples of it. That work belongs to the finished workflow and the people who own it.

The method uses a folding metaphor:

| Term | Plain meaning | What it does for you |
|---|---|---|
| **Sheet** | Project materials | Goal, inputs, people, limits |
| **Creases** | Input/output rules | What the committee notes must look like going in; the action list's skeleton coming out |
| **Folds** | Controlled change | Small, planned, logged edits to the workflow |
| **Unfolding** | Test & fix | Run test cases, inspect, correct |
| **Lock** | Freeze | Make a proven step mandatory |
| **Expose** | Reveal | Add diagnostics so failures are visible |

**Ground rules for this session:**
- We go one stage at a time. Each stage has a summary, an artifact, and your confirmation.
- Missing information is marked **"Unknown / Insufficient data,"** never guessed.
- A chat confirmation moves us forward, but it is **not an approval of record**. Formal approvals (Risk & RACI, Data Handling, Design, Release) need a named approver, the artifact and version, a date, and the word "approved."
- We design exactly **one workflow** this session.
