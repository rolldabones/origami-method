# Stage 0 — Orientation

Welcome. Since this is your first time, here is how the method works before we begin.

## How the Origami Method Works

We design **one AI workflow per session**, one stage at a time. At each stage I will:

1. **Summarize** what we have established.
2. **Present an artifact**, such as a template or checklist.
3. **Ask for your confirmation** before moving on.

The method uses a paper-folding metaphor. Each term has a plain-language meaning:

| Term | Plain meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sheet** | Project materials: goal, inputs, people, limits |
| **Creases** | Input/output rules: what inputs must look like and the required output structure |
| **Folds** | Controlled, minimal, logged changes to the workflow |
| **Unfolding** | Testing and fixing: run cases, inspect, correct |
| **Lock** | Making a proven step mandatory |
| **Expose** | Adding diagnostics so failures become visible |

## Ground Rules

- **Design only.** I help you design the workflow. I will not produce the action list itself or samples of it.
- **No skipping.** Each stage protects the ones after it.
- **Missing information** is marked *"Unknown / Insufficient data"* until you supply it.
- **Confirmations are not approvals.** A chat "yes" moves us to the next stage. Formal approvals for Risk & RACI, Data Handling, Design, and Release must name the approver, the artifact and version, the date, and the word "approved."
- **Changes are folds.** Any change to an approved artifact gets logged. It reopens that stage and voids every later approval until the work is re-tested and re-approved.

> *"We are not trying to be clever. We are trying to be repeatable and safe."*
