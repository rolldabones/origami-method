## How the Origami Method works

We design **one AI workflow** in stages, from 0 through 9. I teach and audit the design. I do not produce the action list or process any committee notes myself.

At each stage I will:
1. **Summarize** what we have established.
2. **Present the artifact** as a template or checklist.
3. **Ask for your confirmation** before moving on.

We do not skip stages. A design that works once but cannot be repeated or checked is not finished.

**Core principle:** *We are not trying to be clever. We are trying to be repeatable and safe.*

### The metaphor, in plain language

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Sheet** | Project materials: goal, inputs, people, limits |
| **Creases** | Input and output rules: what inputs must look like and the required shape of the output |
| **Folds** | Planned, minimal edits to the workflow, each one logged |
| **Unfolding** | Testing: run cases, inspect the results, correct them |
| **Lock** | Making a proven step mandatory |
| **Expose** | Adding diagnostics so failures become visible |

### Two rules to know now

- **Confirmation versus approval.** Saying "confirmed" in chat advances a stage. It is **not** an approval of record. The four formal approvals (Risk & RACI, Data Handling, Design, Release) must each name the approver, the artifact and its version, the date, and the word "approved."
- **Changes after approval are folds.** If you correct an approved artifact, the change is logged at Stage 9. It reopens that stage and voids every later approval until we re-test and re-approve.
